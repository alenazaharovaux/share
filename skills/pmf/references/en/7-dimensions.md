# 7 PMF Dimensions

PMF is not one metric. It is the alignment of 7 dimensions. If even one fails — PMF is not achieved. If all are "fine but not great" — also not achieved.

6 classic dimensions from Marty Cagan + a 7th (Timing/Why Now) from Bill Gross's research.

---

## 1. Problem to Solve

**What good looks like:**
- The problem is **independent of the product** — you can describe it without mentioning your solution
- A concrete outcome the user is trying to achieve + a concrete obstacle
- Emotional charge (annoyance, fear, hopelessness) — means the problem is alive
- You can picture one specific person in the situation, with details

**What bad looks like:**
- "They don't have a good tool for X" (that is not a problem — it is an absence)
- "They are losing money" (too abstract — how much, on what, when)
- "They want more efficiency" (generic — everyone wants that)

**Outcome-Motivation Gap framework:**
1. **What are they trying to achieve?** (outcome — a concrete result)
2. **Why do they want it?** (motivation — what is behind the wish)
3. **What is in the way now?** (gap = problem)

---

## 2. Target Audience

**What good looks like:**
- 2-3 defining attributes — concrete **behavioral/situational** markers (not demographics)
- Now segment separated from Future segments
- You can explain on your fingers why this group specifically (not "because we know them")
- Market size: TAM / SAM / SOM at least roughly

**What bad looks like:**
- Demographic-only ("women 25-45")
- Too broad ("all startups")
- Too narrow without rationale
- Now segment = Future segment (no growth plan thought through)

**Now vs Future:**
- **Now segment** — who is hurting right now, ready to pay, easy to reach
- **Future segments** — where you expand in 1-3 years (usually harder or cheaper/poorer)

---

## 3. Value Proposition

**What good looks like:**
- Tagline = one sentence, **benefit-focused**
- 3-5 sub-benefits, also benefits (not features)
- Benefits answer "what do I get as a user"
- Concrete: "4 hours a week" vs "more time"

**What bad looks like:**
- Features instead of benefits ("integration with 5 platforms")
- Generic claims ("better, faster, cheaper")
- Multiple taglines (no single main value)
- Claims without specificity ("saves time" — how much?)

**Ideal Homepage Approach:**
Tagline → 3-5 sub-benefits → social proof → CTA. If your hypothesis does not fit this format — it needs work.

---

## 4. Competitive Advantage

**What good looks like:**
- One of Helmer's 7 Powers with a concrete compounding mechanism (see `references/<lang>/7-powers.md`)
- Explanation of why this Power works in this context
- A plan for what is needed to build it
- A short competitive landscape (direct + indirect + underserved)

**What bad looks like:**
- "Best team" (not a Power)
- "First to market" (first-mover advantage is rarely sustainable)
- "Unique technology" without a patent moat or process advantage
- Generic "we have better UX"

---

## 5. Growth Strategy

**What good looks like:**
- Short-term traction (first 1K) with a concrete mechanism
- Long-term sustainable (100K+) with a **different** mechanism
- Channels match the business model (not enterprise sales + freemium)
- Owner / cost / expected CAC noted for each channel

**What bad looks like:**
- The same channel for short and long term
- "We will go viral" without a viral mechanism
- "SEO" as short-term (slow channel, does not work at 100 users)
- Paid ads without unit economics

**Two horizons:**
- **Short-term** — manual, founder-led (cold outreach, networks, content, partnerships). Does not scale, but gets the first customers.
- **Long-term** — scalable (paid ads with optimized CAC, viral loops, an SEO content engine, a sales team). Does not work at 100 users.

---

## 6. Business Model

**What good looks like:**
- A concrete business equation for the product type
- Pricing model + concrete price point + rationale
- Cost structure split into fixed/variable
- Path to profitability: how LTV grows vs CAC

**Business equations by type:**

| Type | Equation |
|-----|----------|
| B2B SaaS | LTV > 3× CAC, payback < 12 mo, gross margin > 75% |
| Freemium B2C | (Conversion × ARPU) − CAC > 0, viral coefficient k > 0.5 |
| Marketplace | Take rate × GMV > Cost-to-serve both sides |
| DTC | (LTV × Repeat rate) > CAC + COGS + ops, gross margin > 40% |

**What bad looks like:**
- "There will be ads / freemium / premium" without numbers
- Pricing copied from a competitor without checking it against your audience
- Ignoring cost structure ("it's all software, costs ~0")

---

## 7. Timing / Why Now

**What good looks like:**
- A concrete triggering event (technology, behavior, regulation, economic shift)
- Explanation of why **not a year ago**
- Explanation of why **not in 3 years** (what closes the window)

**What bad looks like:**
- "We feel the time has come"
- "AI hype" (that is noise, not timing)
- A vague "the market is ready"
- No urgency

**Bill Gross research:** after analyzing 200+ companies, he found that timing is the most important factor in startup success (more important than team, idea, business model and funding). Do not skip this dimension.

**Examples of concrete triggering events:**
- A technology became cheap (GPT-4 made translation 100× cheaper)
- A regulation changed (new labeling law starting 2025)
- A generation's behavior changed (Gen Z entered the labor market in volume)
- A platform opened (App Store 2008, iPhone API 2009)
- The economy shifted (downturn → demand for savings vs growth → demand for speed)

## How the dimensions connect

The 7 dimensions are not independent. Strong hypotheses show how they **reinforce each other**:

- **Audience + Problem** — for this specific audience this problem is the sharpest
- **Value Prop + Competitive Advantage** — the value prop cannot be copied thanks to the Power
- **Growth + Business Model** — the channels deliver customers with the right economics
- **Timing + everything else** — what did not work before works now thanks to the shift

Weak hypotheses are the ones where the dimensions are parallel and disconnected.

**Cross-fit conflicts** are checked in Stage 3:
- Channel-Model Fit (Growth vs Business Model)
- Model-Market Fit (Business Model vs Target Audience)
