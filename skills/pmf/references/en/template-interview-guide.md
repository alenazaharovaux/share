## Coverage matrix

Each question covers several dimensions. The goal — across the series of interviews collect data on all 7 dimensions from the narrative.

| Dimension | Covered by block(s) |
|-----------|--------------------------------|
| Customer | Intro, Block 1 (Context) |
| Problem | Block 1, Block 2 (Pain) |
| Why now | Block 2 (Triggers) |
| Why us / Capability | — (not interviewed, validated through experiment) |
| Solution | Block 3 (Reaction) |
| Distribution | Block 4 (Discovery & Decision) |
| Business model | Block 5 (Willingness) |

## Interview structure (45-60 min)

### Intro (3-5 min)

**Goal:** establish rapport, set the rules.

**Script:**

> "Thank you for agreeing! My name is [name], I am working on [one phrase about the project, no deck]. This is research, not sales — I have no goal of selling you anything. We want to understand how [segment] handles [tasks in the area]. The conversation will take about 45 minutes. May I record it for my notes? The recording is not published and not shared with third parties."

**Confirmations:**
- [ ] Recording consent
- [ ] State that there are no "right" answers
- [ ] State that they can interrupt / clarify / refuse to answer

---

### Screening (3-5 min)

**Goal:** make sure the respondent fits the target audience.

**Questions:**
1. Tell me briefly about your role — what your day looks like?
2. How often do you [action from the target audience definition]?
3. [Concrete screening question about the segment]

If the answers do not fit → politely end, thank them, do not continue as "interesting anyway."

---

### Block 1 — Context (10 min)

**Goal:** understand the respondent's work / life context, without leading questions about the product.

**Open questions:**
1. Tell me about the last time you [did the action from the problem space]. When was it? What was happening?
2. What usually triggers [this action]? (trigger)
3. Who else is involved / influences this process?
4. Roughly how much time does it take in a typical week?

**⛔ NO leading:** do not mention the product. Do not mention competitors. Only context.

---

### Block 2 — Pain (10 min)

**Goal:** find a visceral problem wording in their words.

**Open questions:**
1. What in this process annoys you the most?
2. The last time you spent time / money / nerves on it — tell me more.
3. What would you change if you could?
4. (if a workaround was mentioned) Tell me more about how that works for you?

**Listen for:**
- Workarounds (what they already do by hand)
- Frequency markers ("every day", "once a week")
- Severity markers ("this drives me nuts", "I'd pay anything", "it's nothing")

**⛔ Do not propose a solution.** Listen.

---

### Block 3 — Solution Reaction (10 min)

**Goal:** show the product concept (in one phrase, not a demo) and collect the first reaction.

**Script:**

> "Okay, now let me tell you what we are doing — literally in one phrase. [Concept: 'a tool that X']. What do you think about it?"

**Follow-ups (depending on the reaction):**
- (if positive) What exactly grabbed you?
- (if negative) What did not work?
- Who else might find this useful? (uncovers segments)
- What would you want it to do? (uncovers must-have features)
- What would stop you from using it? (uncovers blockers)

**⛔ Do not defend the product.** If a person does not understand — that is your problem, not theirs.

---

### Block 4 — Discovery & Decision (5-7 min)

**Goal:** understand how they make decisions about such tools (for Distribution validation).

**Open questions:**
1. Where do you usually hear about new tools in this area?
2. The last time you tried a new tool — how did you find out about it?
3. What has to happen for you to decide to try something new?
4. Who makes the purchase decision for such tools? (for B2B)

---

### Block 5 — Willingness (5 min)

**Goal:** collect monetization signals, without the direct question "how much would you pay".

**Open questions:**
1. How much do you currently spend on tools in this area (in total)?
2. If such a tool existed and worked — how important would it be for you? (on a scale 1-10)
3. (optionally, carefully) How much do you think such a tool should cost?

**⛔ Do not believe pricing answers literally.** People systematically overestimate their willingness to pay by 2-3x. Use it as a relative signal, not absolute.

---

### Closing (3-5 min)

**Script:**

> "That was the last of my questions. What did I not ask but should have? What would you like to add?"

**Final:**
- [ ] Thank you
- [ ] Ask permission for a follow-up in 2-4 weeks
- [ ] Ask for recommendations for other people to interview (snowball)
- [ ] Promise to share the results if they are interested

---

## Anti-pattern check (before each interview)

- [ ] No leading questions ("Would you agree that our product solves this problem?")
- [ ] No yes/no questions in context blocks
- [ ] No mention of competitors before Block 4
- [ ] No product demo (only the concept in one phrase)
- [ ] No defending the product in Block 3

---

## Quantity target

**Minimum 15 interviews.** Less — not patterns, just individual opinions.

**Ideally 20-25** for:
- 12-15 from the primary segment
- 5-7 from adjacent segments (for persona divides)
- 3 from non-target (for contrast)

**Saturation point:** when the last 3-4 interviews give repeating answers with no new patterns — saturated, you can stop.
